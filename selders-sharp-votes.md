# Selders and Sharp: How Alike Were Their Votes?

*Covers council minutes from December 2023 through November 3, 2025, Lori Sharp's time on the council. Built from `votes.json` (via `pvc votes`) and `pvc ask` over the minutes and packets. Companion to [selders-cole-robinson-votes.md](selders-cole-robinson-votes.md) and [selders-city-hall-votes.md](selders-city-hall-votes.md).*

## Method

The method is the same as in the Cole Robinson comparison:

- **Split votes:** a passed or failed motion where the minutes name at least one dissenter or abstainer.
- **How votes were determined:** a member's vote is **recorded** when the minutes name it. It is **inferred** as yes when the member was on the roll call and isn't among the named dissenters.
- **Tallies** are written yes–no.
- **Names:** the minutes call two members "Robinson". Cole and Dave are counted separately, and the few motions that name only "Robinson" are left out of that comparison.

**Scope:**
- 38 meetings with votes. Sharp attended 35 and Selders 36.
- 91 split votes, 78 of them with both members' votes known.
- Sharp missed 2024-11-18, the Option 2A vote. Selders missed 2025-01-21 and 2025-06-16, two key city hall votes.

### Working partners

Even with all those disagreements, Sharp was Selders's main partner in bringing motions. Of the 33 motions Selders moved or seconded in this period, **Sharp was the other mover or seconder on 15**. Nelson was next with 5. These joint motions had mixed results:
- **Passed:** the resolution clarifying the council's intent for a public vote on a community center (6–5, February 2024), excluding rental and investment properties from home grants (8–1), the Tomahawk Road traffic study (11–1), and the climate-consultant agenda amendment (6–5).
- **Failed:** the Charter Ordinance 28 repeal, the zoning study, the Ordinance 2494 amendment, adding church demolition to the agenda (2–6), and the construction holiday-hours ban (2–9).

## Sharp dissenting alone, Selders with the majority (30 votes)

Most of their differences come from Sharp's steady opposition to taxes, spending and debt. Selders generally voted for these:

- **Tax rate and budgets:**
  - mill levy above the revenue-neutral rate in 2024 and 2025 (four votes, 8–2 to 10–1)
  - the certified 2025 and 2026 budgets
  - the preliminary 2025 budget (11–1)
  - 2025 budget goals
  - 2025 employee pay ranges (9–1)
- **City hall spending:**
  - the 2024 schematic design and the Clark & Enersen Supplemental #2
  - Resolution 2024-07 to buy 7820 Mission and Resolution 2024-08 for temporary notes (both 6–3)
  - the $195K transfer to the project fund (11–1)
  - LEED Gold
  - In Selders's absence, Sharp also cast the lone no on the Option 2A site plan (8–1, January 2025) and voted no on the June 2025 project and bond resolutions.
- **Outside organizations and committees:**
  - human-services and alcohol-tax grants (11–1 each, Dec 2023)
  - the $22,900 Dolyna, Ukraine contribution
  - the Arts Council and Diversity Committee budgets
  - the lobbying contract with Little Government Relations
- **Governance documents:** both Code of Ethics updates (11–1, 10–1) and the 2025 legislative platform (8–1).

## Selders dissenting alone, Sharp with the majority (12 votes)

Selders's solo dissents were about governance, zoning and specific policies, not spending:

- **Council president, December 2023 (Sharp's first month).** Sharp voted against ratifying Selders under the seniority policy (failed 3–8), for Dave Robinson (passed 7–4), and for reviewing the policy. When the revised policy (Council Policy 29) passed in October 2024, Selders was the lone no (11–1) and Sharp voted yes.
- **Zoning:** Ordinance 2494 (Selders the lone no, 12–1) and the R-1B lot recommendation (9–2).
- **Short-term rentals:** the amendment softening "short-term rental prohibited" (passed 8–4).
- **Community center:** the library decision points (10–2).
- **Construction-site signage** (Selders the lone no, 10–1).
- **Safe Streets for All grant agreements, September 2025.** Selders objected to the executive-order language in the federal contract; Sharp voted yes. Both passed 6–4.
- **Drone restriction on the Ukraine contribution (2025-04-21).** Selders moved it and was the lone yes (failed 1–11). Sharp seconded the motion but is recorded voting no.

## Themes

1. **They dissented for different reasons.** Sharp's dissents are mostly about money: taxes, budgets, debt and outside contributions. Selders's are mostly about process, zoning and specific policies, and she voted for nearly every budget and tax-rate resolution in this period. That difference explains why their overall agreement is so low.
2. **They agreed on letting residents decide and slowing big decisions down.** Their joint positions include:
   - putting city hall bonds to a public vote (the Charter Ordinance 28 repeal)
   - ending the community center
   - studying zoning changes before adopting them
   - challenging how agendas are set
   - opposing a fast schedule for the climate guide vote

   On city hall they converged from different starting points. Sharp opposed the spending from the start. Selders supported the plan until the decision to demolish the church. By 2025 both wanted a cheaper renovation (Sharp moved the January 2025 renovation study) and a public vote on the bonds.
3. **They became a two-person bloc in 2025.** Their joint losses doubled, and several 2025 votes were literally Selders and Sharp against everyone else: the Charter Ordinance 28 repeal, the agenda fights and the preliminary 2026 budget. Selders said in May 2025 that she and Sharp had been blocked from putting church rehabilitation on an agenda.
4. **It didn't start as an alliance.** In Sharp's first month she voted against Selders for council president. Their partnership grew out of shared opposition to the city hall and community center process, not a shared fiscal view.

## Compared with Cole Robinson

- **Cole Robinson** agreed with Selders more often (62% in this period) but almost never lost alongside her (1 of 24). He was a frequent majority partner who wasn't there when she lost.
- **Sharp** agreed with her less often (46%) but was with her on half the votes Selders lost, and was her most frequent partner in bringing motions.

## Caveats

- Inferred "yes" votes assume the minutes named every dissenter. An unrecorded abstention would be counted as a yes.
- `votes.json` comes from LLM extraction. One example: the 2025-04-21 drone amendment records Sharp as seconding it and then voting no. That is plausible (a member can second a motion just to allow discussion) but worth checking against the minutes before citing it.
- Split votes are 91 of the 369 motions in this period (25%). On most business, both voted with a unanimous or near-unanimous council.
