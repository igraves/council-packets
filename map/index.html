About this map

This map shows the deed restrictions recorded on Prairie Village's subdivisions, and in particular the racially restrictive covenants that developers wrote into many of them in the 1940s and 1950s, barring Black people from owning or living in the homes.

These covenants are not enforceable. The U.S. Supreme Court held in Shelley v. Kraemer (1948) that courts cannot enforce them, and the Fair Housing Act (1968) and Kansas law (K.S.A. 44-1016 and 44-1017) make housing discrimination unlawful. But the language stays in the public record until it is formally released, and most of it never has been. The purpose of this map is to find it, so that homeowners, homes associations and the city can release all of it under Kansas law: since 2006 a homes association's board can amend a racial covenant out of its declaration, and a 2024 law lets an owner or the city record a certificate releasing one.

Where the documents come from

  • Johnson County Register of Deeds: subdivision plats, declarations of restrictions, homes association declarations, amendments, releases, and deeds for a sample of lots. Each document on the map links to the recorded page images.
  • Johnson County AIMS: the county's public parcel layer, for parcel and subdivision outlines, addresses, and building and land-use details. Owners' names are not shown.

How they are tied to the land

  • Each subdivision is drawn from the county parcels whose IDs carry its subdivision code, so its outline follows the plat's lots and blocks.
  • Restriction instruments were found by searching the recorder's index county-wide by document type and subdivision name, by taking the papers recorded with each plat, and by reading the Miscellaneous filings recorded around each plat's date, many of which were indexed with no legal description.
  • The page images were read with OCR (Tesseract and olmOCR), and the hardest pages by eye. From each declaration we take the blocks and lots it covers, its terms, and any racial language, which is outlined in red on the page images.
  • Some declarations add land to an earlier one rather than repeating its terms: most of Prairie Village was made subject to the 1941 declaration for Blocks 1–5 this way. The map follows those references and carries the covenant to the land they name.
  • Releases were searched for under the homes associations' names and among the certificates of release filed since 2024.

Please note

This is a work in progress and is for informational purposes only. It is not legal advice or a title search, and it may contain errors: OCR misreads, documents not yet found, or a declaration tied to the wrong blocks. Check the recorded documents themselves, and a title professional or attorney, before relying on it for any property.

The full list of documents is in the restrictions report.
