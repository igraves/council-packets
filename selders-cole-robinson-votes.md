# Selders and Cole Robinson: How Their Votes Differ

*Covers council minutes from December 2021 through November 2025, the full period Inga Selders and Cole Robinson served together. Built from `votes.json` (via `pvc votes`) and `pvc ask` over the minutes and packets. Companion to [selders-city-hall-votes.md](selders-city-hall-votes.md).*

## Method

- **Who:** Cole Robinson only, not Dave Robinson. In the minutes he appears as "C. Robinson", "Cole Robinson" or "Robinson, Cole". Six motions name only "Robinson" with no first name. Those were checked and none of them was a split vote, so leaving them out changes nothing. **Sellers** (on the council from December 2025) is a different person from Selders and is outside this period anyway.
- **Scope:** 85 meetings with votes, covering 817 motions. Selders attended 81 of these meetings and Cole Robinson attended 78.
- **Split votes:** a split vote is a passed or failed motion where the minutes name at least one dissenter or abstainer. There were 134.
- **How votes were determined:** most minutes name only the dissenters. A member's vote is **recorded** when the minutes name it. It is **inferred** as yes when the member was on the roll call and isn't among the named dissenters. As in the city hall report, an abstention the minutes didn't record would be missed.
- **Tallies** below are written yes–no. `votes.json` sometimes puts the larger number first on failed motions (e.g. "failed 8-3"), so those have been normalized.

## The numbers

**They agreed on 82 of the 120 split votes where both votes are known (68%), and that rate fell over time:**

| Year | Agreed on split votes | Selders on winning side | Robinson on winning side |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dec 2021 | 3 of 3 | 4 of 4 | 3 of 3 |
| 2022 | 15 of 17 (88%) | 71% | 83% |
| 2023 | 16 of 25 (64%) | 64% | 96% |
| 2024 | 34 of 44 (77%) | 81% | 91% |
| 2025 | 14 of 31 (**45%**) | 58% | 89% |

In 2022 they were close allies who sometimes lost together. By 2025 they agreed on fewer than half of the contested votes. Robinson was on the winning side almost every year. Selders's rate swung by year, and 2025 was her lowest.

## Phase 1 (late 2021 – 2022): frequent allies

Most of their dissents in this period were cast together:

| Date | Motion | Result | Selders | Robinson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-12-20 | Soften the legislative platform's marijuana plank to medical-only | Failed 4–8 | No | No |
| 2022-08-01 | Extend the Bird scooter MOU | Failed 3–7 | No | No |
| 2022-12-05 | Remove rental equipment from the exterior grant program | Passed 8–2 | **No** | **No** (the only two) |
| 2022-12-05 | Same change for the sustainability grant | Passed 7–3 | No | No |
| 2022-12-19 | YMCA MOU for a second community center feasibility study | Passed 7–5 | No | No |

Selders moved the recreational marijuana legalization plank (December 2021, passed 9–3), free COVID test kits for residents, and $10K in radios for Dolyna, Ukraine. Robinson's year was mostly institutional work. He moved or seconded the whole 2022 staff compensation and classification package (12 motions, all unanimous), the parks CIP, budgets, and the human-services grants.

The few early splits between them:
- 2022-01-03: COVID signage messaging plan. Selders yes, Robinson no; failed 5–7.
- 2022-05-02: lengthening a limit in Ordinance 2471 from 14 to 21 days. Selders seconded it, Robinson voted no; failed 3–6.

## Phase 2 (2023): the first real gaps

- **Housing (2023-02-06).** In a three-vote sequence on which zoning recommendations to send to the Planning Commission, Selders "was opposed to removing any items." Robinson moved to restore R-2 but table R-1, saying he backed sending R-2, R-3 and R-4 forward. They split on two of the three votes.
- **Discretionary spending.** Selders cast a run of small-group or near-lone "no" votes:
  - Mayor's allocation for the SME post-graduation party: 9–2.
  - Property tax rebate adjustment: 10–2. Robinson moved it.
  - Foundation donation policies: 10–2.
  - Removing rental homes from grant eligibility: she moved it in December 2023 and it failed 2–9.
- **Ethics complaints (2023-11-20).** Resident Sullinger filed ethics and KOMA complaints against both of them. The complaint against Robinson centered on his job with the Johnson County Democratic Party. The City Attorney found no cognizable claim in either case. Both complaints were dismissed 11–0, and each recused from the vote on their own complaint.
- **Council president (2023-12-18): the sharpest split.** Under the seniority policy, Selders was next in line. Her ratification failed 3–8, and Robinson voted against it. Dave Robinson was then elected 7–4, with Cole Robinson in favor and Selders opposed. Selders and Graves were the only no votes on reviewing the president policy (9–2). When the revised policy (Council Policy 29) passed in October 2024, Selders was the lone no (11–1).

Robinson cast only two recorded "no" votes in all of 2023.

## Phase 3 (2024): splits on the big projects

**City hall.** The full Selders record is in [selders-city-hall-votes.md](selders-city-hall-votes.md). The two of them took nearly opposite positions at each turning point:

| Date | Motion | Result | Selders | Robinson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024-06-03 | Schematic design, City Hall on the south lot | Passed 6–4 | Yes (seconded) | **No** |
| 2024-08-19 | Buy 7820 Mission (the church) | Passed 7–4 | Yes (seconded) | Yes |
| 2024-11-18 | **Option 2A: demolish the church and build new** | Passed 6–5 on the Mayor's tie-break | **No** | **Moved it** |
| 2025-01-21 | Postpone and study renovating the church | Failed 2–7 | Absent | No |
| 2025-06-02 | LEED Platinum for the new building | Failed 4–7 | No | No |

Selders said she wanted more time on the options and opposed changing the church's exterior. Robinson argued for "a new building with a 75-year lifespan rather than renovating a 70 year-old building with a 40-year lifespan," payable "without increasing taxes or affecting its bond rating." He went on to move the 2025 project authorization resolution (Resolution 2025-04, passed 9–2).

**Community center / YMCA.** Both opposed the 2022 YMCA MOU. By October 2024 they had moved apart. Selders voted to "end consideration of the community center project and a partnership with the YMCA entirely" and Robinson voted no; the motion failed 6–7. Earlier that year, in February, Selders had moved the resolution clarifying the council's intent to put a community center to a public vote (passed 6–5).

**Short-term rentals.** Selders wanted a full ban. She called STRs one of the most consistent complaints she heard while campaigning and pointed to Leawood's ban. Robinson "struggled with an outright ban" given "little evidence" that STRs caused more problems than other properties. In September 2024 he moved to study transient guest tax revenue first (passed 9–2, Selders no). In October he and Dave Robinson were the only no votes on sending the ban forward (10–2).

**Zoning.** On 2024-06-17 Selders moved for staff to study allowing residential uses in all commercial districts; it failed 3–9 and Robinson voted no. On 2024-07-01 she moved to strip residential-in-C-2 from Ordinance 2494, which failed 6–7 with Robinson voting with her. She then cast the lone no on the ordinance itself (12–1).

## Phase 4 (2025): the widest gap

They agreed on only 14 of 31 split votes. The splits ran in both directions:

| Date | Motion | Result | Selders | Robinson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-02-18 | Tomahawk Rd speed limit to 25 mph | Passed 7–4 | **Moved it** | No |
| 2025-04-21 | Compost bin pilot (three related votes) | Passed 7–5, 7–6, 8–4 | No ×3 | Yes ×3 (seconded one) |
| 2025-04-21 | Bar drone purchases with the Dolyna, Ukraine contribution | Failed 1–11 | **Moved it** (lone yes) | No (he moved the $22,900 contribution) |
| 2025-06-02 | Preliminary 2026 budget | Passed 9–2 | No | Yes |
| 2025-06-02 | Draft repeal of Charter Ordinance 28 | Failed 2–9 | Yes (seconded) | No |
| 2025-07-07 | EV chargers at the new city hall: users pay for electricity | Passed 9–2 | Yes (seconded) | No |
| 2025-09-15 | Safe Streets for All federal and state grant agreements (two votes) | Passed 6–4 each | No | Yes |
| 2025-09-15 | Agenda fight over the open-discussion item (two votes) | Lost each time | Opposed the majority | Majority |
| 2025-10-20 | Climate consultants' agenda items (two votes) | Passed 6–5 and 8–4 | Won one, lost one | Opposite each time |
| 2025-10-20 | Renew the Tomahawk Rd service station special use permit (Ord. 2511) | Passed 11–1 | Yes | **No (lone)** |

On Safe Streets for All, Selders said the contract's language, specifically "the titles of some of the executive [orders]," did not represent the city's principles. The concern was Section 24.2, which references cooperation with federal immigration officials and rules on diversity, equity and inclusion. On the compost pilot she cited odor, insects and possible food-borne illness on the outside of the bins.

## Themes

### Selders
1. **She frequently ended up in the minority, and outvoted alone.** Three of her votes were lone dissents: Ordinance 2494, the council president policy, and construction-site signage. On the winning side she ranges from 58% to 81% by year.
2. **She cared about procedure and governance.** That covers the seniority-based council president policy, agenda control in 2025, and repeated asks for more time or comparison data before votes (city hall options, the climate action guide).
3. **She went from progressive platform items to fiscal and quality-of-life items.** Her 2021–22 motions were marijuana legalization, COVID test kits and Ukraine aid. From 2023 they shifted toward restraint on discretionary spending (rebates, donations, the 2026 budget, conditions on Ukraine aid, users paying for EV charging), resident complaints (STR ban, construction noise and holiday hours, Tomahawk Rd traffic), and excluding rental and investment property from home grants. The rental-exclusion motion failed 2–9 in 2023 and she won it 8–1 in 2024.
4. **She preferred to preserve and study before building.** She backed the existing-lot city hall plan and buying the church, opposed demolishing it, and wanted residential-in-commercial zoning studied rather than adopted.

### Cole Robinson
1. **He usually voted with the majority.** He was on the winning side 83–96% of the time each year, cast only 2 recorded "no" votes in 2023, and made or seconded nearly twice as many motions as Selders. Most were staff-recommended contracts, budgets, compensation and grants.
2. **He drove the city hall project.** He moved construction-manager and design contracts, Option 2A and the 2025 project resolution, and opposed every attempt to slow it (the south-lot design in 2024, renovation study in 2025).
3. **He was a sustainability and social-policy supporter, but a pragmatic one.** He seconded the KERAMIDA climate plan, backed the compost pilot and expansion, moved the antisemitism resolution and the Ukraine contribution, and seconded the public arts master plan. He also voted against LEED Platinum and against making EV charging users pay.
4. **He was skeptical of new restrictions without evidence.** He opposed the STR ban and wanted tax data first, voted no on the Tomahawk speed cut and the residential-in-commercial study, and cast the lone no on the 2025 service station permit renewal.

### What changed between them
- **2022:** allies on the losing side together (rental equipment, YMCA, Bird).
- **2023:** Robinson joined the governing majority. The council president vote, where he voted against Selders's ratification, is the clearest break.
- **2024:** they split on the big capital decisions. Robinson led on city hall, and they parted on the community center and STRs.
- **2025:** they agreed on less than half of contested votes. Selders was more often in the minority and Robinson stayed with the majority.

## Caveats

- Inferred "yes" votes assume the minutes named every dissenter. An unrecorded abstention would be counted as a yes.
- `votes.json` comes from LLM extraction and can contain errors. The key splits above were checked against the minutes text using `pvc ask`.
- "Winning side" only counts split votes, which make up 16% of all motions. Most business passed unanimously with both of them voting yes.
