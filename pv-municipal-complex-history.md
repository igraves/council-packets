# Prairie Village Municipal Complex: Site Constraints, the Church Property, and the Path Forward

## The Original Problem: A "Tight Squeeze" at 7700 Mission Road

By 2024, the City of Prairie Village had spent four years working toward a municipal complex improvement project at its existing 7700 Mission Road campus. A 2020 ESP Energy Audit identified a grassy field east of City Hall as the site for a geothermal well field to support a ground-source heat pump system (July 20, 2020 Council Packet, p. 97). By 2024, those wells were installed and operational.

Through 2022–2023, Clark & Enersen developed programming and concepts, narrowing from three broad options to Option 3E, then refining it into Option 3E.1 for schematic design. The March 2024 Schematic Design Submittal placed the new City Hall on the south portion of the site where public parking was located, with staff parking positioned east of the building above the existing geothermal well field (June 3, 2024 Council Packet, p. 73). The total project cost was $29.3 million.

Three interlocking constraints made this the only viable arrangement:

- **Geothermal wells** occupied the field east of City Hall, limiting what could be built there
- **Grade changes and existing buildings** blocked other orientations on the north side of the campus
- **South parking lot displacement** — building City Hall on the south lot eliminated a large portion of existing parking, requiring expensive new parking construction and utility relocations

Staff would later characterize this plan as a "tight squeeze" that required costly retaining walls, utility relocations, and new services to modify the site's topography (November 18, 2024 Council Packet, p. 225).


## The Church Goes on the Market (Early–August 2024)

In early 2024, the Mission Road Bible Church at 7820 Mission Road — immediately adjacent to the municipal complex — listed its property for sale. City Attorney Alex Aggen informed the council that the property could be an option for a municipal building or as additional land for the complex (September 3, 2024 Council Packet [August 19 minutes], p. 5).

On August 19, 2024, the council took two critical votes:

- **COU2024-44**: Approved a contract to purchase 7820 Mission Road for $4.5 million with an 18-month lease-back to the church. Passed 7-4 (nay: O'Toole, Agniel, Shelton, Gallagher). (September 3, 2024 Council Packet, p. 6)
- **COU2024-45**: Authorized Clark & Enersen Supplemental No. 3 ($50,000) to evaluate the church property for city use. Passed 10-1 (nay: Agniel). (September 3, 2024 Council Packet, p. 7)

The Clark & Enersen scope letter stated they would "pause all efforts on the current Prairie Village City Hall/PD Renovation/Municipal Court Addition project until this effort is complete" (August 19, 2024 Council Packet, p. 429).


## The Assessment: Changing the Calculus (November 2024)

At the November 4 and November 18, 2024 Committee of the Whole meetings, staff presented the "7820 Mission Assessment and Options," comparing three broad alternatives:

- **Option 1A** — No build at 7820; construct approved project on south lot — $31M
- **Option 1B** — Purchase 7820 for green space; modified project on existing campus — $28M
- **Option 2A** — New City Hall at 7820; PD/Court renovation at 7700 — $31M *(staff recommended)*
- **Option 2B** — City Hall renovation at 7820; PD/Court renovation at 7700 — $32M
- **Option 3A** — New PD at 7820; City Hall/Court at 7700 — $38M
- **Option 3B** — PD renovation at 7820; City Hall/Court at 7700 — $41M

(November 18, 2024 Council Packet, pp. 223–240)

The staff recommendation was emphatic: Option 2A. The presentation explained that the approved project was constrained by high costs from utility relocations, retaining walls, and topography modification — problems the church property simply didn't have. The 7820 site was flat, accessible, and free of the encumbrances that made the south parking lot build so difficult (November 18, 2024 Council Packet, slide 3).

On November 18, Councilmember Cole Robinson moved to proceed with Option 2A. The vote was 6-5, with Mayor Mikkelson casting the tiebreaking vote (December 2, 2024 Council Packet, p. 11).


## The Freed South Parking Lot and the Library Question

Moving City Hall to 7820 Mission fundamentally changed the 7700 Mission campus plan. Instead of demolishing the south parking lot for a new City Hall, the existing campus would undergo only a minimal renovation for Police Department and Municipal Court — preserving the south parking lot and its capacity (January 21, 2025 Council Packet, p. 172 — COU2025-06).

Simultaneously, a parallel track was reshaping the library landscape. The Corinth branch of the Johnson County Library — the oldest in the JCL system, opened in February 1963 — needed replacement (February 2, 2026 Council Packet, p. 9). JCL was evaluating two options: rebuild at its existing Corinth site on Mission Road, or co-locate a new 25,000 sq ft branch with the planned Community Center/YMCA at Harmon Park (October 7, 2024 Council Packet, pp. 90–98).

The JCL's September 16, 2024 letter to City Manager Wes Jordan outlined non-negotiable terms for co-location, including that the city donate 2 acres of land and that the library building require a 2-acre lot (October 7, 2024 Council Packet, p. 94). The Finance Committee voted 6-0 to counter-propose a land swap: the library would receive 2 acres at Harmon Park, and the city would acquire the land the library currently occupies on Mission Road (October 7, 2024 Council Packet, p. 90; October 21, 2024 Council Packet [Oct 7 minutes], p. 6).

The preferred community center concept — Option B, endorsed by Library staff, YMCA, and PV staff — co-located the Community Center/YMCA and Library at the northwest portion of Harmon Park with a shared plaza and generous parking across roughly 10 acres of site development (October 21, 2024 Council Packet, pp. 60–65). A public survey showed 42% support for Option B (North site), versus 25% for Option A (South site) and 26% for no changes (October 21, 2024 Council Packet, p. 57).

The church acquisition resolved the parking pressure that had been the central constraint of the original plan. With City Hall no longer consuming the south parking lot, the existing 7700 Mission campus retained its full parking capacity for the PD/Court renovation. And the broader community center campus at Harmon Park — with the library co-located — could proceed with sufficient dedicated parking without competing for spaces in an already-strained civic corridor.


## Financing and Construction (2025–2026)

In early 2025, the city issued GO temporary notes to acquire 7820 Mission Road. On June 16, 2025, the council authorized GO bonds for the project. A lawsuit delayed bond issuance (filed in state court, withdrawn, refiled in federal court, then dismissed), but on December 1, 2025, the council approved bonds not to exceed $28 million. The final par value was $25,625,000, and bonds closed December 23, 2025 (January 5, 2026 Council Packet, pp. 53–60).

The city takes possession of the church property on April 1, 2026, with asbestos remediation and construction mobilization to follow. A GMP contract is expected for council approval on March 2, 2026 (January 5, 2026 Council Packet, p. 57).
