## 2027 budget (adopted)

Staff presented the 2027 budget as holding the mill levy flat at 18.057 mills (after a reduction the prior year), citing uncertainty from possible state property-tax legislation and the pending expiration of the county public safety sales tax (~$570,000 loss) as reasons not to cut the rate further. The General Fund 'all-in' total of $34,538,314 represents a 3.4% increase over 2026, driven mainly by continued implementation of the 2022 compensation study and 2024 police market adjustments, full CIP transfer funding, and debt service transfers for the Municipal Complex and Public Works Facility. The overall certified budget across all funds totals $52,081,648 in budget authority, with an ad valorem tax levy of $13,815,370, exceeding the revenue neutral rate of 16.973 mills, which required a public hearing and resolution before adoption on September 8, 2026.

- Contract Services: $42,928 (+0.7%), 4% of the change

## 2026 budget (adopted)

Staff describe total expenditure growth of about 2% over 2025, driven by a 3.4% rise in personnel costs from continued Salary Study implementation and benefits, a 4.5% increase in contract services, and an 11.6% jump in capital/infrastructure spending tied to major street and stormwater projects, partly offset by a large drawdown of contingency reserves in pass-through funds. The Governing Body directed staff to maintain a General Fund balance of at least 25% of revenues, and the 2026 budget meets this target with a projected 28.04% reserve. ## 2025 budget (adopted)

The 2025 budget, adopted September 3, 2024, keeps the citywide mill levy flat at 18.326 mills even though that exceeded the state's 17.365-mill revenue neutral rate, triggering a required public hearing. Staff framed overall spending growth of about 2% as driven mainly by a 6.6% rise in personnel costs tied to police salary increases and continued implementation of the 2022 Salary Study, partially offset by a large drop in capital/ARPA-funded spending that had inflated the 2024 budget. The Governing Body directed staff to maintain a General Fund balance equal to at least 25% of General Fund revenue, and the 2025 budget's projected 25.15% reserve meets that target while continuing a declining five-year trend from 41% in 2023. Infrastructure funding fell 13% from 2024 levels as municipal complex project spending wound down, while Meadowbrook TIF pass-through debt payments rose 24%.

## 2020 budget (adopted)

The 2020 adopted budget for Prairie Village held the mill levy flat at 19.314 mills while increasing property tax revenue 7.8% due to rising assessed valuations, and grew sales/use and franchise fee revenues modestly amid a strong local economy. Staff and Council framed the budget as prudent and balanced without a tax increase, relying on 2018 budget savings to close an initial revenue-expenditure gap while continuing to fund the 2017 Comprehensive Salary Plan (4% merit pool) and rising KPERS pension costs. Capital infrastructure remained a priority, consuming about 23% of total expenditures and 19.5% of General Fund resources, funded largely by General Fund reserves in excess of the 25% minimum balance target.
