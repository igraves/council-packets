# Graves Compared with Selders, Cole Robinson and Sharp

*Covers council minutes from January 2020 through August 2026. Built from `votes.json` (via `pvc votes`) and `pvc ask`. Companion to [selders-cole-robinson-votes.md](selders-cole-robinson-votes.md) and [selders-sharp-votes.md](selders-sharp-votes.md).*

## Method

The method is the same as in the companion reports:

- **Split votes:** passed or failed motions where the minutes name at least one dissenter or abstainer.
- **How votes were determined:** a member's vote is **recorded** when the minutes name it. It is **inferred** as yes when the member was on the roll call and isn't among the named dissenters.
- **Tallies** are written yes–no.
- **Names:** the minutes call two members "Robinson". Cole and Dave are counted separately, and the few motions that name only "Robinson" are left out of that comparison.

**Graves's record:**
- He served the whole period, starting the same month as Selders (January 2020). That's 133 meetings in the data.
- His vote is known on 273 split votes. He was on the winning side of 236 of them (86%), compared with 74% for Selders (209 of 284).
- He made or seconded 209 motions, most often with Nelson (41), McFadden (31) and Shelton (17).

## Graves and Selders

### Where they agreed (often in the minority)
- **Home grant program (January 2021).** Both voted to rename it the "home grant program" and open it to interior improvements; it failed on two 6–7 votes.
- **Marijuana legalization plank (December 2020).** Both supported changing the platform from medical marijuana to legalization (failed 4–7).
- **Property tax rebate (2021).** Both voted to raise the rebate's maximum property value (failed 4–5).
- **Council president (December 2023).** Graves moved to ratify Selders under the seniority policy and defended that policy as ensuring experience and spreading presidents across wards. Both lost (3–8) and both opposed the policy review (9–2).
- **Housing recommendations (February 2023).** Both voted against removing the R-1, R-2 and by-right references (passed 6–5).
- **Safe Streets for All (September 2025).** Both voted no on both grant agreements (passed 6–4 each).
- **Bird scooter pilot (2021).** Both voted against the one-year pilot MOU (passed 7–6). They split on the 2022 extension (Graves yes, Selders no).

**Note on Resolution 2024-01 (2024-02-20 minutes, pp. 6–7; packet, COU2024-13).** The resolution was about whether the council intended to hold a public vote. It did not decide whether a vote would be held.
- **What it said:** staff drafted a version stating the council "intends to put the decision regarding the funding and approval of the currently considered civic improvement of a new community center to a public vote, as allowed by law."
- **What Selders and Sharp first proposed:** a stronger red line that said the decision "shall only be" made by public vote and "should be and will be decided by a public vote," "preferably at a special election this calendar year."
- **Why it was softened:** Gallagher objected that this read as requiring a vote even if the council decided not to build. City Attorney Aggen agreed.
- **What passed:** Selders withdrew her motion and re-moved the red line with each disputed phrase replaced by the staff wording ("desires to affirm its intention," "would be intended to," "intends to") and the special-election clause dropped. That passed 6–5 over Nelson, Wolf, Shelton, Graves and Gallagher.
- **Shelton's alternative:** before the vote, Shelton had circulated another version he called "a more precise accounting of what the council was doing and what steps would come next."
- **Why Graves voted no:** the minutes don't say.
- **The vote was required anyway:** by October 2024 the plan was to pay for the roughly $55M project with G.O. bonds repaid by a 5/8% to 3/4% city sales tax increase. Under K.S.A. 12-187 that increase requires a public vote. The Finance Committee recommended planning for that vote, and Graves made the motion to proceed (passed 8–4). When the project was cancelled, Graves and Nelson voted no and, with the Mayor, said they wanted it to go to a public vote.

## Graves and Cole Robinson

They agreed 77% of the time over Cole Robinson's whole tenure, and 89% in 2025. Both:
- backed the city hall project through Option 2A and opposed the January 2025 renovation study
- supported the community center (with one difference: Robinson voted for the October 2024 motion ceasing all work, Graves against)
- were in the majority on the 2025 compost, climate guide and agenda votes
- opposed repealing Charter Ordinance 28

Their main differences:
- **COVID mitigation, 2021–22:** Graves supported the mask-mandate and signage motions; Robinson voted no on each.
- **The council president vote:** Robinson voted against Selders; Graves moved her ratification.
- **Safe Streets for All:** Graves voted no; Robinson yes.
- **Smaller 2024–25 items:** LEED Platinum (Graves yes, Robinson no), the Ordinance 2499 owner-occupied exception (Graves yes, Robinson no), and final Ordinance 2499 (Graves no, Robinson yes).

## Graves and Sharp

They agreed on only 17 of 72 split votes (24%), falling to 5 of 33 in 2025. The split is mostly about money and city hall:

- **Taxes and spending:** Sharp's routine no votes on mill levies above the revenue-neutral rate, budgets, pay ranges, outside-group funding and city hall spending were mostly on motions Graves moved or seconded. These include the 2025 budget goals, the 2026 Arts Council and Diversity Committee budgets, the LEED Gold direction and the 2026 budget.
- **City hall:** Sharp wanted a cheaper renovation of the church and a public vote on the bonds. Graves seconded Option 2A and voted against both her January 2025 postponement and the Charter Ordinance 28 repeal.
- **Process:** on the 2025 agenda and climate-guide fights, Graves moved or voted with the majority, and Sharp and Selders were the minority.
- **They never lost together.** All 17 of their agreements were on the winning side, mostly lopsided votes where a third member dissented (for example Ordinance 2494, 12–1, and the CP29 revision, 11–1). On every split vote where at least one of them lost, they were on opposite sides. That includes the December 2023 council president vote: Graves moved Selders's ratification and Sharp voted against it.

Over 2020–2025, Graves moved from Selders's camp to Cole Robinson's. In the pandemic years he and Selders were frequent allies on progressive policy items, though they split on mask mandates. In 2023 he still backed her for council president. From 2024 he sided with the majority on the community center, city hall, zoning and agenda control, while Selders aligned more with Sharp.

## Caveats

- Inferred "yes" votes assume the minutes named every dissenter.
- `votes.json` comes from LLM extraction and sometimes writes failed tallies with the larger number first. Key votes here were checked against the `pvc votes` output and the minutes.
- The Cole Robinson and Sharp "winning side" figures come from the companion reports' windows. Graves's and Selders's cover all of 2020–2026.
- Two different members are recorded as "Nelson" (Nelson and J. Nelson), so the Nelson agreement figure may combine more than one person.
