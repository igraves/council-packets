# Prairie Village Municipal Complex: The Existing Lot, the Church Property, and the Path Forward

*Covers council packets and minutes from 2022 through the September 8, 2026 packet.*

Citations take the form **(YYYY-MM-DD packet|minutes, p. N)**. The date is the meeting's file date in `pv_council_docs/`, and N is the PDF page index. Many minutes are reprinted as drafts in the next meeting's packet; where possible, citations point to the standalone minutes file. Minutes record what was said and voted on at council. Packets hold staff and consultant material.

## Summary

- **2022 to July 2024: only the existing lot was studied.** The council considered building City Hall only on its existing 7700 Mission Road campus. The adopted design put a new City Hall on the south surface parking lot.
- **July 2024: the church appears.** The adjacent Mission Road Bible Church at 7820 Mission Road first came up as a possible City purchase in July 2024. The council agreed in August to buy it for $4.5M.
- **November 2024: the church lot is chosen.** On November 18, 2024, after comparing six options, the council directed staff to build a new City Hall on the church lot (Option 2A). The vote was 6-5 and needed the Mayor's tie-break. The police department (PD) and municipal court would be renovated at 7700 Mission.
- **2025: every attempt to revisit it failed.** Later efforts to reopen the choice were defeated, most notably a January 2025 motion to study renovating the church instead of demolishing it.
- **2026: construction under way.** A lawsuit delayed the bonds, which closed in December 2025. The construction contract's guaranteed maximum price (GMP) was approved in April 2026 under a $30M total project cap. The groundbreaking was July 10, 2026, and foundations were going in by mid-August 2026.


## Phase 1: Building on the Existing Lot (2022 – July 2024)

### Needs and early options

A February 2022 staff memo listed City Hall's problems: staff split up and working in converted closets, no private staff restrooms, one conference room, and no room to add staff (2022-02-12 packet, p. 4). In September 2022 the council hired Clark & Enersen for $130,000 to do a facility assessment and programming through schematic design. The motion passed unanimously (2022-09-06 minutes, pp. 7–8).

In June 2023 Finance Director Hannaman said the budget could carry about $20–25M of debt. Borrowing $20M would cost about $1.12M a year and $33M about $1.68M a year (2023-06-20 minutes, p. 5).

The 2022 assessment found "no structural issues that warrant a full building replacement," but it did find aging mechanical, electrical, and sewer systems (2023-07-17 packet, p. 51). Staff presented three options. The cost slides are scanned, so these figures come from OCR:

| Option | Description | Approx. cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Basic renovation | $11.9M |
| 2 | Heavy renovation and addition | $21.8M |
| 3A | New construction, including about $1.3M for south parking | $30.8M |

(2023-07-17 packet, pp. 52–68, esp. p. 64)

On July 17, 2023, the council voted 9-0 to proceed with Option 3 Phase A. The Finance Committee had recommended a $30M ceiling. The new City Hall would be "placed on south end of parking lot" (2023-07-17 minutes, pp. 3–4).

In August 2023, Supplemental No. 1 with Clark & Enersen added $360,000 for concept and schematic design, bringing the total to $490,000. The scope covered a new City Hall, demolition of the existing building, LEED Platinum, and a $30M budget. It passed unanimously (2023-08-21 minutes, pp. 4–5; 2023-08-21 packet, p. 35).

### Option 3E and schematic design

On November 6, 2023, staff presented five new-build layouts (2023-11-06 packet, pp. 167–182):

| Option | Cost range |
|---|---|
| 3A | $31.5–32.4M |
| 3B | $30.5–31.3M |
| 3C | $31.4–32.2M |
| 3D | $26.7–28.2M |
| 3E | $28.6–30.2M |

Staff recommended 3E, which moved the court into an addition to the police building. Councilmember comments:

- Gallagher asked whether the court legally had to stand alone.
- Limbird preferred 3D.
- The Mayor and D. Robinson suggested reusing the existing chamber as the court.

Option 3E passed 11-0 (2023-11-06 minutes, pp. 8–10). The packet describes 3E as single-story, but the minutes call it a "two-story building with centered Council chamber."

In January 2024 two add-ons were costed:

- Keeping the existing chambers for the court: $29.5M, against $28.6M for the base plan.
- Adding underground parking: $34.5M. The garage would have needed access "off of the driveway to the south which is not on city property."

(2024-01-16 packet, p. 127)

The council declined both, 11-0 (2024-02-05 packet, pp. 12–13).

In March 2024 the council hired a construction manager and an owner's representative:

- McCown Gordon as construction manager, 10-0.
- Project Advocates as owner's representative, 9-1 (nay: Agniel).

(2024-03-04 minutes, pp. 5–6)

The March 2024 schematic design, Option 3E.1, had two phases:

- **Phase 1:** an 18,148 sq ft City Hall "on the south end of campus on what is today an asphalt surface parking lot," with staff parking "above the existing geothermal well-field."
- **Phase 2:** demolish the old City Hall, add a 6,648 sq ft court addition, and renovate the MPR wing of the existing building for the police department.

The total cost was $29,331,420 (2024-06-03 packet, pp. 73–74, 82). The site narrative also mentions a sidewalk "providing a connection for church overflow parking" (p. 82).

### Pushback

Residents spoke against the new City Hall at the April 1, 2024 meeting (2024-04-01 minutes, p. 2). At the April 2, 2024 Planning Commission:

- Commissioner Birkel questioned putting City Hall "at the bottom of the hill."
- Commissioner Brown wanted the southeast parking pushed back from Mission Road.
- Commissioner Breneman said the design didn't fit Prairie Village's style.

(2024-05-20 packet, pp. 66–68)

At a March 26 public meeting, 17 of 31 comment cards opposed the plan (2024-06-03 packet, pp. 13–14).

On June 3, 2024, architect Rick Wise said other locations on the campus had been studied and the proposed one was "most ideal." Councilmember comments:

- Gallagher wanted a grander entry.
- Selders found the design too modern.
- Sharp wanted lower costs.
- D. Robinson asked whether a heavy remodel was still possible. City Engineer Prenger said it would cost about the same, do less for the police department, and require moving staff out.

(2024-06-03 minutes, pp. 3–5)

The votes that night were close:

- Schematic design passed **6-4** (nay: C. Robinson, O'Toole, Sharp, Agniel).
- Clark & Enersen Supplemental No. 2 ($2,049,000) passed **7-3** (nay: O'Toole, Sharp, Agniel).

(2024-06-03 minutes, pp. 3–5)

### When the church entered the picture

Before July 2024 the church appears in the records only incidentally:

- As a map label (2021-01-19 packet, p. 164).
- As overflow parking for a fire-station open house (2024-05-20 packet, p. 72).

The first link to City Hall is on July 15, 2024. The Mayor reported "a meeting with Mission Road Bible Church to discuss the potential purchase of the property by the City," and the council held two executive sessions on acquiring real property (2024-07-15 minutes, pp. 3, 7). Earlier real-property executive sessions (April 1 and May 20, 2024) don't name the property.


## Phase 2: Buying the Church and Choosing the Site (August – December 2024)

### Purchase contract

The contract terms (2024-08-19 packet, pp. 413–416):

- Price $4,500,000, with $45,000 earnest money.
- A 120-day due-diligence period, closing by February 15, 2025.
- The church could lease the building back for up to 18 months, ending no later than April 1, 2026, at $1 a year. It would pay taxes, upkeep, insurance, and utilities.
- The sale depended on the church closing on its new property in Shawnee.

On August 19, 2024 the council voted on two items:

- **COU2024-44, the purchase contract:** passed **7-4** (nay: O'Toole, Agniel, Shelton, Gallagher). O'Toole said residents and developers told him $4.5M was too high. Gallagher raised the fire district's driveway across the property (2024-08-19 minutes, pp. 3–4).
- **COU2024-45, Clark & Enersen Supplemental No. 3 ($50,000):** a study of the church site. Passed **10-1** (nay: Agniel) (2024-08-19 minutes, pp. 4–5). Clark & Enersen paused work on the approved project while the study ran (2024-08-19 packet, p. 429).

### The 7820 assessment and options

Staff presented the assessment at the November 4 and November 18 Committee of the Whole meetings. Totals include the police department and court (2024-11-18 packet, p. 236):

| Option | What it meant | City Hall | Court | PD | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1A | Approved project on the existing lot; no land purchase | $19M | $8M | $4M | $31M |
| 1B | Approved project modified, with a basic court renovation in the existing building | $19M | $5M | $4M | $28M |
| **2A** | **New City Hall at 7820 (land included); PD and court renovated at 7700/7710** | $23M | $4M | $4M | **$31M** |
| 2B | Renovate the church building as City Hall (40-year life) | $24M ($27M with addition) | $4M | $4M | $32M |
| 3A | New police building at 7820; City Hall moves into the police building | $5M | $4M | $29M | $38M |
| 3B | Police in the renovated church | $5M | $4M | $32M | $41M |

The November 4 memo defined the options differently (2024-11-04 packet, p. 35; 2024-11-04 minutes, pp. 6–7):

- 1A was "$0, don't purchase."
- 1B was "$6M, purchase and make it green space." Either was paired with the $31M approved project.
- 2A was given as $31–35M and 2B as $32–38M.

The $28M figure for 1B in the November 18 table was added because councilmembers asked to see the cost of a basic court renovation (2024-11-18 packet, p. 236).

**Staff's case for Option 2A:**

- The existing-lot plan was a "tight squeeze." It required costly utility relocations, new services, and retaining walls, and was best only "when there are no other options" (2024-11-18 packet, pp. 226, 237).
- The church lot had none of those problems (p. 226).
- 2A stayed within the approved budget ceiling and "should not affect the AAA bond rating" (pp. 237–238).
- City Administrator Jordan said building on the church lot would cost about the same as building on the parking lot (2024-11-04 minutes, p. 7).

**Constraints on the church lot:**

- The fire district keeps its driveway across the property.
- A county sewer main "cuts the property in half."
- Asbestos abatement was estimated at about $135K.

(2024-11-18 packet, pp. 229–231)

**Why staff rejected renovating the church (2B):**

- The church is 30,000 sq ft, against an 18,200 sq ft new building.
- A renovation has a 40-year life and can cost as much as or more than new construction.

(2024-11-04 minutes, p. 6; 2024-11-18 packet, p. 237)

**Why staff rejected Option 3:** there is not enough parking at 7820 for the court (2024-11-18 packet, p. 246).

**Debt:** Hannaman said a $31M project meant borrowing about $27.2M, costing about $1.52M a year in debt service. That was $373.7K more than was already funded (2024-11-04 minutes, p. 7).

### November 18, 2024 votes

Councilmember arguments (2024-11-18 minutes, pp. 5–8):

- **For the church lot:**
  - C. Robinson said a new building lasting 75 years was better than renovating a 70-year-old building that would last 40 years, and it needed no tax increase. He also warned that renegotiating could put the sale at risk.
  - Shelton said the climate action plan called for a new LEED building.
  - D. Robinson said the purchase decision was due because due diligence was ending.
- **Price and process:**
  - O'Toole objected that the City had no realtor representing it.
  - The Mayor said a realtor would add no value at that stage.
  - Staff said the church had a competing offer and would not renegotiate.
- **Against:** Selders opposed changing the church's exterior and wanted more time.

The motions that night:

| Motion | Result | Dissent |
|---|---|---|
| Hire a realtor on contingency to renegotiate the price (O'Toole / Agniel) | Failed 3-7 | For: O'Toole, Agniel, Reddell |
| Extend due diligence 30 days with a $5K deposit (C. Robinson / Selders) | Passed 8-2 | Nay: O'Toole, Reddell |
| Engage a financial advisor and bond counsel (C. Robinson / Shelton) | Passed 8-2 | Nay: O'Toole, Agniel |
| **Direct staff to proceed with Option 2A (C. Robinson / Graves)** | **Passed 6-5 on the Mayor's tie-break** | Nay: Selders, Nguyen, D. Robinson, Agniel, Reddell |

Sharp and Gallagher were absent, so the ten councilmembers present split 5-5 and Mayor Mikkelson broke the tie (2024-11-18 minutes, pp. 1, 8). The minutes don't name the aye votes. This was guidance given in Committee of the Whole; a later staff report calls it "motioned and approved" (2024-12-02 packet, p. 69).

### December 2024

On December 2 (2024-12-02 minutes, pp. 1–6):

- **Resolution 2024-07**, authorizing the acquisition: passed **6-3** (nay: O'Toole, Sharp, Reddell).
- **Resolution 2024-08**, authorizing up to $5M in temporary notes: passed **6-3**, with the same three opposed.
- Sharp moved to put the total project cost to a public ballot. He supported the purchase itself. He withdrew the motion after Gallagher objected that it lacked specifics (2024-12-02 minutes, pp. 3–4; 2024-12-16 minutes, p. 2).
- Public comment ran mostly against the project.

On December 16, Selders moved to add a discussion of demolishing the church to the agenda. It failed **2-6**, with only Selders and Sharp in favor (2024-12-16 minutes, p. 1).


## Phase 3: Attempts to Revisit, Financing, and Litigation (2025)

### January 21: the last formal challenge

The council voted on the purchase financing and on the site plan (2025-01-21 minutes, pp. 6–8; 2025-01-21 packet, pp. 98–99, 172):

- **Temporary notes:** Resolution 2025-02, authorizing notes to buy the church, passed 9-0.
- **Site plan presented:** staff presented the 2A plan under a $30M ceiling. The building was made 20 feet shorter to clear the sewer easement.
- **Sharp's motion to postpone:** Sharp moved to delay two weeks and explore a cheaper renovation of the church. D. Robinson seconded. Jordan said staff had already studied renovation. Shelton said renovating would cost as much as new construction, fail to meet program needs, and have a shorter lifespan. The motion failed **2-7** (aye: Sharp, D. Robinson). **This is the only formal motion in the records to revisit the site choice.**
- **Site plan approved:** the 2A site plan passed **8-1** (nay: Sharp).

No motion in 2025 or 2026 proposed returning to the existing lot or selling the church property.

### Spring 2025

- **Closing:** the City closed on the church property in early 2025 (2025-03-03 minutes, p. 4).
- **Planning Commission, March 4:** commissioners Birkel and Herron asked why the building wasn't placed nearer the existing campus or rotated. The architect cited the council's wish for it to face Mission Road, the geothermal lines, and grade changes (2025-04-07 packet, pp. 195–197).
- **May 5:** the project was discussed again (2025-05-05 minutes, pp. 3, 5, 7–8):
  - Gallagher asked whether residents could vote. City Attorney Aggen said an advisory election isn't allowed, and a binding bond vote would require replacing Charter Ordinance 28. Graves noted the public works building was also built without a vote.
  - Selders said she and Sharp had been blocked from putting church rehabilitation on an agenda. The Mayor cited the failed 2-7 vote.
  - C. Robinson said no plan had shown the police department could expand without a new City Hall.
  - A $195,000 transfer to cover an ARPA shortfall passed 11-1 (nay: Sharp).

### LEED level (June 2)

Costs were $283K for LEED Silver, $335K for Gold, and $644K for Platinum. Platinum failed **4-7** (aye: Nelson, Nguyen, Shelton, Graves); the minutes misprint it as "7-4." Gold passed **8-3** (nay: O'Toole, Sharp, Agniel) (2025-06-02 minutes, pp. 4–6).

### Authorization (June 16)

The design-development estimate was $30.0M, down from $31.0M in November 2024 (2025-06-16 minutes, p. 4; 2025-06-16 packet, p. 24):

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| PD renovation | $3.0M |
| PD site work | $1.5M |
| Court renovation | $2.6M |
| City Hall | $15.5M |
| City Hall site work | $2.9M |
| Land | $4.5M |
| **Total** | **$30.0M** |

Three measures passed, each **9-2** (nay: Sharp, Reddell):

- Resolution 2025-04, authorizing the project.
- Resolution 2025-05, authorizing GO bonds up to $30M.
- An amendment to the debt policy.

(2025-06-16 minutes, pp. 5–8)

### Lawsuit and bonds

- **July 7:** City Attorney Aggen reported a lawsuit seeking to stop the new City Hall (2025-07-07 minutes, p. 7).
- **August 4:** a federal judge denied the City's request to expedite, so no bonds were sold (2025-08-04 minutes, p. 3).
- **November 3:** the Mayor reported that the lawsuit had been dismissed (2025-11-03 minutes, p. 5).
- **December 1:** the newly seated council passed **Ordinance 2514** 11-0 (2025-12-01 minutes, pp. 4–5). It authorized Series 2025A GO bonds up to $28M and retired the $4,745,000 temporary notes.
- **Outcome:** final par was $25,625,000, and the bonds closed December 23, 2025 (2026-01-05 packet, p. 54).


## Phase 4: Construction (2026)

- **January 5:** Finance Director Hannaman presented the plan: approve the GMP on March 2, then take possession of the church and begin asbestos work on April 1 (2026-01-05 minutes, p. 4).
- **March 2:** the GMP was not on the agenda. Hannaman said debt service would reach $1.6M a year from 2028 (2026-03-02 minutes, p. 5).
- **April 6:** the council approved Amendment No. 1 with McCown Gordon, setting the **GMP at $20,442,340**. It passed **11-1** (nay: Reddell, no reason recorded) (2026-04-06 packet, pp. 409–412; 2026-04-06 minutes, pp. 6–7).
  - Bids had come in "higher than anticipated." The City delayed a month and cut more than $500K, including one diesel generator instead of two natural-gas units.
  - The overall budget holds to "the $30 million cap established for the project" (2026-04-06 packet, p. 410). It includes $12.18M for the new City Hall building and $2.85M for its site work.
  - Asbestos abatement (INSCO, $160,850) and air monitoring (Axiom) contracts passed unanimously (2026-04-06 minutes, pp. 7–8). Axiom's proposal schedules abatement of the *existing* City Hall in 2027 (2026-04-06 packet, p. 467).
- **Schedule in the GMP** (2026-04-06 packet, pp. 413, 442):
  - PD substantial completion: March 1, 2027.
  - New City Hall substantial completion: June 3, 2027.
  - Courts substantial completion: November 30, 2027.
  - Project complete: December 2027, which staff described as "early 2028" (2026-04-06 minutes, p. 7).
- **Demolition:** a resident's June 15 comment refers to the church "being demolished for new city facilities" (2026-06-15 minutes, p. 2).
- **July 10:** groundbreaking (2026-07-20 minutes, p. 4).
- **August 17:** staff reported that the MPR-wing remodel of the existing City Hall was nearly complete and that footings and foundations for the new City Hall were being installed (2026-08-17 minutes, p. 3).

No council debate on the site choice appears in 2026. Objections came only from members of the public; on August 3, for example, Rosberg suggested alternative locations (2026-08-03 minutes, p. 1).

**Still open:** the documents contain no stated plan for the old City Hall building or the south parking lot. Demolition of the existing north paving is scheduled for mid-2027 (2026-04-06 packet, p. 442). Separately, the Johnson County Library presented a test-fit for a new Corinth branch on the community-center site at 7720 Mission on June 15, 2026. The council took no action (2026-06-15 minutes, p. 4).
